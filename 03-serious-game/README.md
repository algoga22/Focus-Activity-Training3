# Challenge 3 – Serious game: "Put out the fire"

**In one sentence:** the analogy of the training, *"an incident is like a fire"*, becomes the rule of a team game where every second of hesitation makes the fire grow and every classic mistake is petrol.

## How it works

- **The board:** our NGO has **4 rooms**, each with a real incident:
  - work phones: a phone lost on the bus;
  - social media: the Facebook page posts things nobody wrote;
  - beneficiary data: a list sent to the wrong person;
  - office computers: ransomware.
- **4 rounds = 4 rooms.** Each round has **4 phases**, which are the 4 steps of the rule:
  1. *Is it a fire?* (recognise)
  2. *Cut the fire* (stop and disconnect)
  3. *Call the firefighters* (report)
  4. *Fire report* (write down)
- **Each phase** is a decision on the phone with 3 options: **one right move (water)** and **two trap cards (petrol)**, which are the classic mistakes and the misconceptions of the quiz (pay the ransom, restart and see, delete the evidence, wait until Monday, say nothing…).
- **One fire per room for the whole team.** The fire grows 1.6 % per second while people answer. When the phase closes it goes down with the share of right answers (up to −40 %) and up with the share of trap cards (up to +30 %).
  - A room that ends under 50 % is **saved**; otherwise it is **damaged**.
  - At 100 % it **burns down** and the fire **spreads**: the next room starts at 45 % instead of 20 %.
- **Surprise:** in the last room a "hacker" message from "Anna (management)" asks to pay and to keep it secret.
- **Points for everyone:** 100 for a right move + up to 50 for speed, with a leaderboard after each room.
- **After each phase**, the main screen shows the answers, the effect on the fire (waiting, water, petrol) and a one-sentence explanation; the phone says "Water!" or "Petrol!".

**Timing (15'):** 1' rules (slides 20–21) + about 10' of play (16 phases of up to 25 s, plus results) + about 4' debrief.

**The trainer only clicks "Start the round" and "Next room":** phases close by themselves when everyone has answered or after 25 seconds, and the next phase starts after 9 seconds (**Pause** stops the countdown).

## Why it is a good game for this training

- **Players apply the rule, they don't recall it:** 16 decisions in 4 different incidents, in the order of the 4 steps, under time pressure.
- **All the learning objectives:** LO1 (phase 1), LO2 (phase 2), LO3 (phase 3), LO4 (phase 4) and LO5 (the trap cards, in every phase).
- **Original:** the fire analogy of the slides becomes the mechanic. Time really costs ("the sooner, the better") and mistakes really make things worse (petrol).
- **Emotions help memory:** team spirit (one fire for everyone), competition (points), tension (the timer, the spreading fire) and surprise (the hacker message).
- **Quick and accessible:** rules in 30 seconds and the same QR app as the quiz, with no account and no download. Participants who joined for the quiz are already in, and the game works online and onsite.

## Objective evidence

At the end, the debrief screen shows:
- **rooms saved** (out of 4);
- **% of right moves per objective** (LO1–LO4 by phase, LO5 = trap cards avoided), next to the result of the **quiz at the start** for the same objective;
- the **most chosen trap cards**, i.e. the misconceptions that remain;
- the **best firefighters**, the answers of every person, and a **CSV** download.
