# Challenge 4 – Learning by doing

**In one sentence:** a hands-on exercise at the end of the session where participants solve a realistic NGO case on their own, which also **shows what they have learned** during the training.

> **Status:** to do. The structure (alone → groups → debrief, 14') is fixed. **The case still has to be chosen.** The ransom screen, the lost phone and the hacked Facebook page are already used in the slides and the game, so the final case should be a **new situation**, to check that participants can transfer the 4 steps (Stop, Disconnect, Report, Write down).

## Purpose
- **Practise ONE critical skill**, the most important one of the training: taking the right first steps when something goes wrong.
- Participants **find the solution themselves** (deciding, analysing, solving) instead of listening.
- **Measure what participants have learned:** each person first answers alone, so the trainer can see who has understood and compare with the quiz at the start ([Challenge 2](../02-learner-assessment/)).
- **Build autonomy:** after the exercise they can do it alone in real life.

## When in the session
**41–55 min (14 minutes)**, after the theory and the game ([Challenge 3](../03-serious-game/)):
- **5'** alone: each participant answers the case on a short sheet (or an online form / the chat)
- **5'** in groups: compare answers and agree on the best response
- **4'** debrief: correct steps, common mistakes and comparison with the start

## Requirements
- **Not a game.** Use a group debate, an exercise or a case analysis.
- Focuses on **one** key learning objective.
- **Realistic NGO scenario** (real constraints: small team, no IT department, volunteers, sensitive beneficiary data…).
- **Clear instructions:** scenario, steps, expected result and guidance for the trainer.
- **Easy to score:** expected answers are defined in advance, so each sheet can be marked good / partial / wrong.
- **Debrief:** review answers and mistakes together and explain the correct behaviour.
- Feasible in the time with simple materials (paper sheet onsite, shared form or chat online).

## Simple examples

**Case analysis: "Friday afternoon at the food bank"**
> It's Friday at 17:00. Marie, a volunteer, opens an email "Invoice overdue" and clicks the attachment. Her screen goes black with a message asking for €500 in Bitcoin. The coordinator has already left and there is no IT person.

Each participant fills in a simple sheet **alone**:
1. What is the first thing Marie should do?
2. Who should she tell, and how, if the coordinator isn't there?
3. What should she write down?
4. What should she **not** do?

**Expected answers (for the trainer):** 1. Stop and disconnect from the internet · 2. Call the backup contact, don't wait until Monday · 3. What happened, when, a photo of the screen · 4. Pay, delete the email, switch off the computer, hide it.

**Groups and debrief:** groups compare their sheets and agree on one answer, then the trainer shows the correct steps and explains the common mistakes. A quick show of hands ("Who got question 1 right? And in the quiz at the start?") makes the progress visible to everyone.

**Other possible cases:** a lost phone and a hacked Facebook page were also ideas, but they are now rooms of the game (Challenge 3).
