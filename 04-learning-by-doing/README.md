# Challenge 4 – Learning by doing: "The urgent payment"

**In one sentence:** each participant applies the four steps of the training, on their own, to an incident they have never seen: a fake email from the NGO's president that makes the treasurer pay €2,000 to criminals.

**When:** minutes 41–53 (12 minutes), after the game and just before the final quiz.

## The skill we practise

**Apply the first steps (Stop, Disconnect, Report, Write down) to a new incident, without help.** This is the core of the training: everything else (recognising an incident, avoiding mistakes) is useful only if people act correctly in the first minutes.

We chose a **new situation** on purpose. The ransom screen, the lost phone, the hacked Facebook page and the email sent to the wrong person were already used in the quiz, the slides and the game. Here nothing is broken on the computer, there is no virus and no IT problem, and participants must still recognise the incident and apply the same rule. This shows whether they have understood the rule or only memorised examples.

The case is realistic for a small NGO: volunteers with real responsibilities, no IT department, the coordinator away, pressure to keep things quiet, and donors' money at stake. Fake "urgent payment" emails that pretend to come from a manager are a common scam against small organisations.

## The case

*Food for All* is a small food bank: 2 employees, 40 volunteers, no IT department. **Sofia** is a volunteer and the treasurer.

| Moment | What happens | Question |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Tuesday 16:40** | An email from "Paul Martin, President", sent from a **gmail.com** address, asks Sofia to pay **today** a €2,000 invoice of a "new supplier" and to **keep it confidential**. Sofia pays at 16:55. At 17:10 she notices that Paul always writes from **@foodforall.org**. | **Q1.** Is this an incident? What should Sofia do in the first minutes? |
| **2 · 17:15** | Sofia calls Paul on his usual number: he never sent the email. The coordinator is on holiday until Monday. Sofia feels guilty. A colleague says: *"Reply and ask them to send the money back. And let's keep this between us."* | **Q2.** Who should Sofia tell, how, and how fast? **Q3.** What should Sofia NOT do? |
| **3 · 17:40** | The bank's fraud team calls back: to try to stop the payment they need precise information. | **Q4.** What should Sofia have written down? |

## How to run it (12 minutes)

| Time | Step | What the trainer does |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Introduction (slides 22–23) | Hand out the case sheet. Online: paste the PDF in the chat. Say that this is practice, not an exam. |
| 0:30 | **4' alone** (slides 24–26) | Show moment 1, give about 1 minute for Q1. Then moment 2 (about 1.5 minutes for Q2 and Q3), then moment 3 (1 minute for Q4). Do not help and do not comment yet. |
| 4:30 | **4' in groups of 3–4** (slide 26, second click) | Groups compare their sheets, agree on one answer per question and choose a spokesperson. Online: breakout rooms. Walk around and note the mistakes you hear. |
| 8:30 | **4' debrief together** (slides 27–28) | For each question: ask one group, then show the answer, then a quick show of hands ("Who wrote: call the bank?"). Use the common mistakes you heard. |

**Materials:** one printed case sheet per person (or the PDF online), a pen, the slides. No technology needed apart from the projector.

## Answer key and scoring

Each answer is scored **good (2) / partial (1) / wrong (0)**: maximum 8 points. The trainer can collect the sheets (nickname only) to score them.

| Q | Objective | Good (2) | Partial (1) | Wrong (0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Q1** | LO1 Recognise, LO2 First steps | **Yes, it is an incident** (money may have gone to criminals) **and** stop: no other payment, don't reply, don't click, keep the email | Says it is an incident but no first step, or a first step without seeing it is an incident | "Not an incident", "wait and see", or pay / reply |
| **Q2** | LO3 Report | Call **the bank now** (the payment can sometimes be stopped if the bank knows within minutes) **and** the contact person or the backup **today, by phone** | Only one of the two, or by email, or "tomorrow" | Wait until Monday, or tell nobody |
| **Q3** | LO5 Avoid mistakes | At least 2 of: don't **reply** to the fraudster, don't **hide** it, don't **delete** the email, don't **pay** again | 1 of them | None, or recommends one of them |
| **Q4** | LO4 Write down | At least 3 of: **time** of the email and of the payment, **sender address**, **amount and bank details**, **who she called and when**, keep the email / screenshot | 1–2 of them | Nothing useful ("there was a problem") |

**What the four steps mean in this case** (said in the debrief): **Stop** = no other payment, no reply. **Disconnect** = nothing to unplug: "closing the door" here means asking the bank to stop the payment. **Report** = the bank and the contact person, now, by phone. **Write down** = the facts the bank needs.

## Debrief: key messages

- **Speed is money.** As with the fire, the first minutes decide: a payment reported quickly can sometimes be stopped.
- **Nobody is to blame.** These emails are made to fool people. Hiding out of shame ("let's keep this between us") is what turns a small fire into a big one.
- **Never answer the fraudster.** Check by calling the real person on a number you already know.
- **The four steps work for every incident**, even when nothing is broken on the computer.

Common mistakes to look for: waiting for the coordinator on Monday, replying to the email to ask for the money back, deleting the email out of embarrassment, reporting only by email.

## How the case connects to the rest of the session

- It uses the **same rule and the same words** as the slides (minutes 18–26), the game and the [cheat sheet](../05-take-home/), whose "Quick decisions" box includes "Fake invoice paid: call the bank now, then your contact".
- Each question is linked to a learning objective, so the trainer sees on the sheets which step is still weak. Right after the case, the [same quiz as at the start](../02-learner-assessment/) measures the progress of the whole group (minutes 53–56).
